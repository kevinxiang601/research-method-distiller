# Idea Formation

Use this file when the user asks whether a real-world phenomenon can become a publishable paper, or asks for ideas in supply chain, platform, IO, AI, policy, or Chinese-context research.

## Core Logic

A strong idea is not a hot topic. It is a real decision tension that breaks an assumption in prior theory and leaves observable traces.

Use this chain:

1. real anomaly or decision pain;
2. old literature's hidden assumption;
3. new mechanism that changes incentives, information, timing, or constraints;
4. minimal model or minimal empirical design;
5. insight that changes a managerial or policy decision.

## High-Value Tension Types

- Dual identity: platform as both referee and competitor; manufacturer as supplier and downstream entrant; government as regulator and market creator.
- Negative shock with positive opportunity: export control, supply disruption, platform ban, data restriction, or environmental regulation may both constrain and incentivize substitution or innovation.
- Information structure change: certification, disclosure, verification, review bias, search friction, privacy rights, or analytics changes who knows what.
- Responsibility boundary: recall liability, inventory responsibility period, platform disintermediation responsibility, data error liability, or carbon responsibility changes behavior.
- Algorithmic intervention: algorithms alter timing, commitment, speed, targeting, fairness, or observability, not just efficiency.
- Context-to-theory: Chinese policy, local government, state-market relations, industrial policy, and platform regulation matter only when they change objectives, constraints, legitimacy, or resource access.

## Idea Screening Questions

Ask these before proposing a paper:

1. Who makes a real decision, and what trade-off do they face?
2. Which prior assumption fails here?
3. Does the new mechanism change payoffs, information, timing, entry, matching, or learning?
4. Can the mechanism be shown in a two-player model, a minimal dynamic model, or a clean empirical design?
5. What data trace would exist if the mechanism is real?
6. What result would be surprising enough to matter?
7. Who changes what decision after reading the paper?

## Common Weak Ideas

- Merely applying a known model to a new industry without changing the mechanism.
- Using China only as a background sample without explaining which assumption China changes.
- Asking whether X affects Y without a reason for sign ambiguity or mechanism tension.
- Adding AI, blockchain, platform, or ESG as labels without changing information, incentives, or constraints.
- Listing many mechanisms but not building one clean core mechanism.

## Output Pattern

For each candidate idea, write:

- Reality: the concrete scene.
- Tension: why common intuition is incomplete.
- Gap: which literature assumption fails.
- Mechanism: the one core moving part.
- Model route: players, choices, information, payoffs.
- Empirical route: shock/key X, outcome, sample, identification.
- Data route: where the observable trace may come from.
- Contribution: what theory or decision changes.
- Risk: why this may fail.
