# Top-Journal Writing

Use this file when drafting or revising titles, abstracts, introductions, literature reviews, model/method sections, results, managerial implications, conclusions, or contribution paragraphs.

## Writing Principle

Imitate rhetorical moves, not sentences. Keep the English natural, precise, cautious, and mechanism-driven. Do not copy full sentences from papers.

## Title

A strong title usually compresses:

- object: platform, supply chain, algorithm, disclosure, search, data, regulation;
- mechanism: information, learning, competition, responsibility, risk, coordination;
- result or tension when helpful.

Avoid vague titles that only say X and Y.

## Abstract

Use the chain:

1. problem or puzzle;
2. method or setting;
3. main finding;
4. mechanism;
5. boundary condition;
6. contribution or implication.

Good abstracts do not list every robustness test. They make the reader understand why the result matters.

## Introduction

Use this sequence:

1. concrete real-world context;
2. decision tension;
3. why existing theory cannot fully answer it;
4. what the paper does;
5. main findings in plain English;
6. contribution to literature;
7. managerial or policy implications.

Make the tension early. Avoid starting with broad claims such as technology is important or supply chains are complex.

## Literature Review

Organize by conversations, not by authors. Use moves such as:

- we differ by changing the information structure;
- we complement by showing the boundary condition;
- we extend by adding strategic response;
- we reconcile mixed findings by separating mechanisms;
- we use a different empirical setting because it reveals an otherwise hidden mechanism.

## Model Or Method Section

For model papers, introduce assumptions as translations of reality. For empirical papers, define treatment/exposure so clearly that another researcher can reconstruct it.

Do not hide the key idea inside technical notation. Put the mechanism in words before equations or regressions.

## Results

State the result first, then give intuition.

Use cautious but confident language:

- The result suggests...
- This pattern is consistent with...
- The mechanism operates through...
- The effect is concentrated among...
- These findings are difficult to reconcile with...

## Managerial Implications

Write implications as decisions:

- who should act;
- what they should change;
- under what condition;
- what trade-off they should watch.

Avoid generic implications such as managers should pay attention to the issue.

## Conclusion And Limitations

Return to the original tension, state what was learned, and define boundaries. Strong conclusions are restrained; they do not oversell.
